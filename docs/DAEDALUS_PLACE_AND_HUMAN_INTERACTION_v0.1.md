# Daedalus Place and Human Interaction Twin v0.1

## Status

Sandbox product expansion proposal grounded in the existing Living Twin model.

## Canonical expansion

Daedalus should treat a **Place** as the broader spatial root while preserving Property as a valid and important kind of Place.

A Place may contain:

- one or more buildings;
- rooms and internal circulation;
- paths, roads, parking and external terrain;
- temporary layouts and operational obstacles;
- services, environmental conditions and communication systems;
- people, groups, equipment and tasks moving through it.

The Digital Twin remains the product.

## Product families

Potential product framing:

- **Daedalus Home** — individual homes and residential properties;
- **Daedalus Places** — shops, holiday parks, hotels, attractions, leisure, education, healthcare and public venues;
- **Daedalus Estate** — multiple buildings, outdoor routes, infrastructure and operational conditions across a managed estate.

These are deployments of the same Twin concept, not unrelated applications.

## Human Interaction Twin

Human Interaction replaces the narrower concept of an Accessibility Twin.

Purpose:

> Explain how a selected person, group, item of equipment or task can perceive, reach, move through and interact with a real place.

It must not produce a universal accessible/not-accessible score.

## Interaction profiles

Profiles are explicit parameter sets, not stereotypes or permanent labels.

Examples include:

### Mobility

- manual wheelchair;
- powered wheelchair;
- mobility scooter;
- rollator;
- walking frame;
- walking stick;
- crutches;
- reduced walking endurance;
- reduced balance;
- limited reach;
- limited grip or opening force.

### Perception and sensory interaction

- blind navigation;
- low vision;
- colour-vision difference;
- hearing impairment;
- reduced contrast sensitivity;
- sensory sensitivity to noise, crowding, glare or visual complexity.

### Family and equipment

- single, double or triple stroller;
- wagon;
- child carrier;
- luggage;
- shopping trolley or basket;
- parent or carer supporting children;
- carrying a baby or other load.

### Group and task

- family/group profiles;
- staff moving a stock cage;
- cleaner or maintenance worker moving equipment;
- emergency evacuation;
- visitor trying to reach a specific activity or facility.

Profiles may combine. For example, a group may include two adults, four children, multiple pushchairs, luggage and a requirement to avoid high-noise areas.

## Capability envelope

Each profile should define the relevant envelope, such as:

- width and length;
- turning radius;
- gradient limits or effort assumptions;
- step and threshold tolerance;
- reach height and reach depth;
- required transfer space;
- door-opening force tolerance;
- visual contrast or lighting needs;
- sound or sensory constraints;
- permitted surface types;
- required rest intervals or seating proximity.

Daedalus explains outcomes against the selected envelope. It does not claim legal certification unless a separate verified standard and evidence process explicitly supports that statement.

## Spatial results

A route or interaction may resolve as:

- **reachable** — supported by verified geometry and conditions;
- **constrained** — possible but with tight clearances, effort, sensory or operational limitations;
- **blocked** — stopped by a known object, route condition or capability limit;
- **unknown** — insufficient evidence to claim passability or interaction;
- **temporarily unavailable** — normally possible but unavailable under current operational state.

Results should expose:

- route distance;
- extra distance compared with the direct route;
- narrowest verified clearance;
- steepest gradient;
- thresholds and steps;
- lift, ramp or assistance dependency;
- turning or transfer constraints;
- environmental hot/cold/noisy/dark/glare zones;
- unavailable equipment or facilities;
- supporting evidence and uncertainty.

## Environmental and communication projections

The same place Twin can project spatial conditions such as:

- cold spots, hot spots and overheating zones;
- draught paths;
- Wi-Fi coverage and dead zones;
- cellular coverage by network or band;
- poor lighting, glare or low-contrast areas;
- noisy areas and quiet/sensory refuge routes;
- air quality, humidity and ventilation state;
- crowding, queues and temporary pinch points;
- flood, drainage or surface-condition constraints.

Each projection uses the same spatial model, evidence rules and visual language.

## Public venue page

A venue may publish a public, evidence-backed Daedalus page.

Suggested opening question:

> What are you bringing, and what matters to you?

Public flow:

1. choose people, equipment and relevant needs;
2. choose a destination, facility or activity;
3. watch the route or interaction animate through the Place Twin;
4. inspect distance, extra distance, effort, barriers, environmental conditions and uncertainty;
5. save or share the result;
6. see current operational changes where available.

Examples:

- Can this stroller reach the pool without stairs?
- How much farther is the step-free route?
- Will this wagon fit through the shop and arcade routes?
- Where is cellular coverage strongest?
- Which route avoids the loudest areas?
- Can a person using crutches reach the accessible toilet without a long unsupported walk?

## Daedalus place badge

The badge represents transparency and traceable evidence, not perfection.

Potential levels:

### Daedalus Captured

The Place has a traceable spatial Twin with evidence and visible gaps.

### Daedalus Verified

A defined set of routes, facilities, environmental conditions or interaction profiles has been checked against a published evidence standard.

### Daedalus Live

The public Twin includes current operational state, for example:

- lift unavailable;
- route blocked;
- high crowding;
- facility closed;
- temporary event layout;
- unusually hot or cold zone;
- current Wi-Fi/cellular outage or degradation.

Preferred badge meaning:

> This place publishes a transparent, evidence-backed Twin that visitors can test against their own needs and equipment.

Do not use wording that implies universal suitability.

## Commercial value

A venue or estate may pay for:

- Capture and Twin creation;
- public interactive pages;
- verification and revalidation;
- operational-state updates;
- an embeddable booking or venue widget;
- multi-site estate management;
- API access for tourism, retail or booking platforms;
- analysis of failed routes, missing evidence and frequently tested profiles;
- customer, staff, facilities and emergency-use projections.

## Boundaries

Human Interaction and public Place pages must remain explanatory.

They may show:

- constraints;
- consequences;
- route differences;
- evidence gaps;
- temporary state;
- what is reachable for a selected profile.

They must not silently:

- claim universal accessibility;
- replace formal legal or regulatory assessment;
- hide unknown measurements;
- invent routes through unverified geometry;
- recommend products or mobility equipment;
- rank venues as a generic answer;
- treat one person or device as the definition of accessibility.
